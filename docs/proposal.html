01 · Executive summary

Know every machine.
Prove every licence.

Signet gives the county a simple, reliable way to see which gaming machines are licensed, check them on site in seconds, and act on those that are not.

Every licensed cabinet receives two tamper-evident QR tags: one on the front panel and a hidden backup inside the locked door. Each tag carries only a digitally signed link — no personal data. A county verifier scans the tag with a phone, and Signet confirms instantly whether the machine is licensed, which premises it belongs to, whether the licence is active or suspended, and whether the tag is genuine or a copy.

Every scan is recorded with the officer, time and place. The county gets a live register of every machine, a clear trail of every inspection, and the evidence it needs to collect revenue and enforce its by-laws.

02 · The challenge

Paper licences are easy to fake.

Today, most counties rely on printed certificates and stickers to show that a gaming machine is licensed. These are hard to check and easy to abuse.

Stickers are copied

A printed sticker or certificate can be photocopied and placed on an unlicensed machine. On site, an officer cannot easily tell the difference.

Licences move between machines

A licence paid for one cabinet is moved to another, or one licence covers several machines.

Suspended machines keep running

When a licence is suspended, the machine often stays in use because there is no quick way to check its status on site.

Records are scattered

Registers live in files and spreadsheets. It is hard to know how many machines exist, where they are, and when each was last inspected.

Inspections leave no trail

There is little record of who inspected which machine, when, and with what result — which weakens enforcement and invites disputes.

Revenue leaks

Every unlicensed or under-declared machine is a licence fee the county does not collect.

The result: lost revenue, weak enforcement, exposure of the public — including minors — to unregulated gaming, and little data to plan with.
03 · How it works

Register. Tag. Scan. Act.

Signet is a web-based system with two parts: a county register of every licensed machine, and a phone app that verifiers use to check machines on site.

1

Register

The county records the machine: licence number, premises, ward, operator and cabinet serial.

2

Tag

Signet issues two signed QR tags. They are fixed to the cabinet — front and inside the locked door.

3

Scan

A verifier opens the app and points the phone camera at the tag. The result appears in seconds.

4

Act

The scan is logged. Problems are flagged for follow-up: notice, suspension or seizure.

Two tags on every cabinet

① Primary tag — front panel

About 8 cm wide, placed clear of the screen and the door seam so it is easy to scan and cannot be lifted with the door. This is the tag verifiers scan on every visit.

② Backup tag — inside the locked door

Hidden inside the cabinet. If the front tag is damaged, removed or replaced with a fake, the officer has the operator open the door and scans the backup. Because it is locked away, it is very hard to copy or swap.

What is on the tag

Only a short signed code, for example S1.CG-2026-00731.primary.4f9a…. It names the licence and which tag it is, followed by a digital signature that only the county system can produce. Change a single character — or print a code for a licence that does not exist — and the signature no longer matches. No names, phone numbers or ID numbers are ever printed on the tag.

04 · On site

Every scan gives a clear answer.

The verifier sees a large, colour-coded result with the machine's details, and knows exactly what to do next.

ResultWhat it meansWhat the officer does
SealedGenuine front tag, licence active, details match the cabinet.Confirm the serial and premises match. Visit recorded. No action needed.
Backup verifiedThe backup tag inside the door is genuine and active.Record the visit and note that the front tag needs replacing.
Retired tagThe tag was genuine once but has been replaced. Someone is using an old tag.Scan the backup tag. Investigate why the old tag is still on a machine.
MismatchA genuine tag, but it belongs to a different cabinet or premises.Possible licence transfer. Record the case and check the serial plate.
SuspendedThe licence is genuine but currently suspended.The machine must not operate. Issue notice or seal the machine per by-law.
ForgedThe signature is wrong. The tag was not issued by the county.Treat as unlicensed. Photograph, record and begin enforcement.
UnknownThe code is not a Signet tag at all.Look for the real tag or backup. If none, treat as unlicensed.

If a machine has no tag at all

A machine with neither tag is treated as unlicensed until proven otherwise. The officer asks the operator to open the door for the backup tag. If there is none, the officer looks up the cabinet serial in the register. If the machine is registered, it is re-tagged; if not, enforcement begins.

05 · Benefits

Why it matters for the county.

Revenue protection

Every operating machine must carry a valid, signed tag. Unlicensed and lapsed machines become visible, and fees are collected in full. A copied tag cannot cover a second machine, because the cabinet details will not match.

Faster, fairer enforcement

Officers decide on facts, not on how a sticker looks. Results are the same for every operator, and each decision is backed by a recorded scan.

Accountability and anti-corruption

Each scan is tied to a named officer, time and place, and cannot be edited afterwards. Supervisors can see who checked what, and spot visits that never happened.

A live register

One up-to-date list of every machine, premises and operator, with search, filters and status — replacing scattered files and spreadsheets.

Data for planning

See compliance by ward, find hotspots of suspended or unlicensed machines, and plan inspections where they matter most.

Public protection

Suspended and illegal machines are found and stopped sooner, reducing exposure of the public and minors to unregulated gaming.

Lower cost

Runs on ordinary smartphones officers already carry. No special scanners. Tags are cheap to print and replace.

Fair to operators

Licensed operators can prove compliance instantly. Inspections are quicker and less disruptive to legitimate businesses.

06 · Security & privacy

Built to be trusted.

Signed tags

Each code carries a digital signature made with a secret key held only by the county system. Forged and altered tags are detected instantly.

Two-tag design

The backup tag inside the locked door means a damaged or swapped front tag never leaves a machine unverifiable.

Retire and replace

Lost or damaged tags are retired in the system. Any later scan of a retired tag is refused and flagged.

No personal data on tags

Tags carry a signed link only. Personal information stays in the secured county system, in line with the Data Protection Act, 2019.

Officer accounts and roles

Only signed-in officers can verify machines. Licensing staff, verifiers and supervisors see only what their role allows.

Complete audit trail

Every registration, tag change, suspension and scan is logged permanently with who, when and where.

Built for the field

Any smartphone

Runs in the phone's web browser. Nothing to install from an app store.

Seconds per check

One tap opens the camera; the result appears as soon as the tag is in view.

Hosted securely

Encrypted connections and hosting, with daily backups of the register.

08 · Implementation

Start small. Prove it. Scale.

We recommend a pilot in one or two wards, followed by county-wide rollout once results are confirmed.

Weeks 1 – 4 · Set-up

Configure and load data

Agree licence rules and by-law workflows with the county. Import the existing machine register. Create officer accounts. Set up secure hosting and signing keys.

Weeks 5 – 8 · Tagging

Tag machines in the pilot wards

Print tamper-evident tags. Officers fix front and backup tags to every licensed cabinet, registering serials as they go.

Weeks 9 – 20 · Pilot

Inspect and measure

Officers run routine inspections with Signet. We track machines found unlicensed, revenue recovered, time per inspection and officer feedback.

Month 6 · Review

Report to the county

Pilot results and recommendations presented. Decision on county-wide rollout.

Months 7 – 12 · Rollout

County-wide and Phase 2 features

All wards tagged. Enforcement map, GPS and photo evidence, offline mode and dashboards added.
